Front page Planning · Retrofit first · Heritage · Paddington

Heritage harm outweighed by “enormous” economic benefits: Westminster councillors overrule their own officers and approve HOK’s Paddington life sciences hub, which will replace the former Imperial College Medical School

The Architects’ Journal (26 November) reports that Westminster City Council’s planning committee voted four to one on Tuesday evening (24 November) for HOK’s 16-storey, 48,800m² clinical life sciences hub beside St Mary’s Hospital, although planning officers had recommended refusal on environmental and heritage grounds.

The scheme, for Bartlet Asset Management, replaces the former Imperial College Medical School, designed by Edwin Cooper in 1933 and later extended, which sits between Praed Street and South Wharf Road and has been empty since 2024. Historic England said the plans would cause “considerable harm” to the Bayswater Conservation Area through the loss of the building and the new block’s “incongruous bulk, scale and massing”; the Twentieth Century Society argued that a scheme reusing the existing building should be considered under the council’s own retrofit-first policy, and the Royal Parks also objected. The committee concluded that the economic case outweighed both the heritage harm and the environmental cost of demolition. Councillor Rachael Robathan said she “completely understand[s] the officers’ concerns”, but that the result would be “a building fit for the future”; the officers’ report itself cited potential tax revenue of more than a billion pounds over ten years and up to 2,000 operational jobs.

HOK’s case for demolition is technical rather than aesthetic. Design principal David Weatherhead told the AJ that the practice “always prioritise[s] a ‘retrofit first’ approach”, but that after “deep investigation into its structure, systems and condition” the building was judged “beyond its useful life”. The design documents list a lack of step-free access, an inability to carry modern laboratory services and vibration from nearby Tube lines that rules out MRI and CT equipment. The new building targets BREEAM Outstanding and expects to divert more than 98 per cent of construction waste from landfill. Read after Monday’s National Gallery wing and Wednesday’s London Museum, it completes a week-long argument: when a reuse policy meets a client with a laboratory brief, the policy becomes one consideration to be weighed rather than a rule.

CRIT · Question of the day

If a retrofit-first policy can be outweighed whenever the new use is valuable enough, is it a policy about buildings, or a price list for demolition?

AI · Technology · Infrastructure

Opinion · Liability · Insurance (25 Nov)

Audit yourself before your client’s chatbot does: an architect argues that practices should run AI over their own drawings and appointments — and that insurers currently punish them for trying

In an AJ opinion piece (25 November), Toko Andrews, an associate at James Clague Architects and associate lecturer at the University for the Creative Arts, starts from the PHP flaws that AI helped researchers uncover after 14 years in the code. Clients, she warns, could run the drawings, specifications and appointment letters they have been issued through a large language model and find faults the architect never noticed; she cites Lord Briggs’s warning of a “tsunami” of AI-assisted claims. Her answer is to use the same tools first, starting with appointments, which the ARB lists as the most common subject of complaint.

The obstacle is the insurance market. Insurers typically require notification of any issue that could lead to a claim, so “the practice that looked may end up paying more than the one that didn’t”. Andrews notes that the Insurance Act 2015 already requires a “reasonable search of information”, that the Defective Premises Act’s long limitation period makes project history matter, and that AI “hallucinations” will always need a human check. She asks the ARB and the RIBA to set out how practices can carry out deep AI audits “without fear of penalty”.

CRITThe usual AI story is about producing drawings faster. This one is about reading old drawings more suspiciously. If a model can find the fault you missed in 2019, what does “professional judgement” mean at the moment you sign the drawing today?

Biennales · Curating · Seoul (26 Nov)

“One assistant curator isn’t even human”: Carlo Ratti is named curator of the 2027 Seoul Biennale of Architecture and Urbanism and promises an AI-based approach

Dezeen (26 November) reports that Seoul mayor Oh Se Hoon has appointed the Italian architect Carlo Ratti to direct the sixth Seoul Biennale, succeeding Thomas Heatherwick, who curated the 2025 edition. Ratti says he will work with an AI assistant and use the event to examine what a curator’s role is today, highlighting emerging tools that can help curators develop exhibitions.

The detail is still thin: the title, theme and full curatorial team are due to be announced on 8 December, and the biennale opens on 24 September 2027. The article does not say how the AI assistant will work. For readers in Seoul, it is the rare London-reported architecture story that lands at home.

CRITA curator selects, frames and excludes. If one member of the curatorial team is a model, whose exclusions are they, and who answers for them when an exhibitor asks why they were left out?

Europe · Museums · Reuse · Brussels (26 Nov)

“Trust the building as we found it”: Kanal opens this Saturday in Brussels’s 1934 Citroën garage, with three new volumes inside the old structure and the city’s architecture museum stacked in one of them

Dezeen (26 November) reports that Kanal, the museum of modern art and architecture on the Brussels canal, opens to the public on 28 November in a garage and showroom built for Citroën in 1934. The transformation is by Atelier Kanal, a consortium of EM2N, NoAarchitecten and Sergison Bates Architects. It restores the industrial building and inserts three volumes: a four-storey gallery block, a block with a 378-seat auditorium and offices, and a nine-storey structure for Kanal Architecture, the city’s architecture museum and archive formerly known as CIVA. The museum covers 40,000m², and its programme was developed with the Centre Pompidou; Assemble designed an indoor playground.

Atelier Kanal describes the project as an attitude of “radical optimism” rather than “a spectacular gesture”: “our ambition has been to trust the building as we found it, embracing its industrial character and many layers of use.” Large parts of the building are meant to be used by citizens and community groups, so that the museum “extends beyond curated art, becoming part of the city and its fabric.”

CRITThe same Saturday, London and Brussels open museums in former market and garage halls. Both keep the shed and put new rooms inside it. Draw the two long sections side by side: is “the box in the shed” now a strategy, or a house style for reuse?

Opinion · Infrastructure · Maintenance · Bristol (26 Nov)

“When maintenance succeeds, nothing happens”: after the central section of Bristol’s Pero’s Bridge collapsed, the president of the architectural technologists asks whether the failure was structural or a failure of the system

Writing in the AJ (26 November), Usman Yaqub, president of the Chartered Institute of Architectural Technologists, notes that major structural issues with the footbridge were first found in July 2025 and that it had been closed since. Bristol’s Vauxhall Bridge is under extensive repair and other crossings on the New Cut are part of a multi-million-pound programme of works to eight bridges. Why, he asks, are they all being repaired at once: were routine works deferred until small problems began to snowball?

His argument is about what the profession celebrates. New buildings have “programmes, budgets, photographs and opening days”, but “there is no ribbon cutting for replacing a bearing before it fails”. Handover, he writes, is “just the beginning of a structure’s life”; maintenance skills should be treated “as infrastructure in their own right”. The council still intends to repair the bridge and reopen it in spring or summer 2027.

CRITEvery Diploma portfolio ends at completion. What would a project look like if its final drawing were the maintenance schedule at year 40, and its client the person who replaces the bearings?

Feature · New towns · Policy (26 Nov)

New towns without a champion: Andy Burnham has not used the phrase since taking office, a decision on the seven candidate sites slips to next year, and designers argue over whether devolution helps or kills the programme

An AJ feature (26 November) notes that the government has pushed back its decision on which, if any, of the seven possible locations will go ahead, and that the Financial Times has reported the prime minister “cooling” on his predecessor’s top-down approach in favour of devolved housing powers. There is no dedicated budget: housing minister Matthew Pennycook told a Lords committee in September that setting one would be “replicating what is already there”, so projects must bid into existing funds. Jon Ackroyd of Ackroyd Lowrie warns that without development corporations with “real vision and some teeth”, “they’ll have a general election, they won’t have built anything”.

Others see devolution as an opening. Arup’s Sowmya Parthasarathy, a member of the New Towns Taskforce, says that “the real design quality can only be ensured, monitored and delivered at the town level”, and YTL’s Sebastian Loyn, whose Brabazon site in Bristol already has more than 400 homes built, says “the people closest to a place are often best placed to unlock its potential”. The fear, for the critics, is drift; for the developers already on site, it is interference.

CRITStevenage was drawn by a development corporation with a single client. Parthasarathy says you cannot deliver great architecture without “the right client”. Who is the client of a new town that is funded by competitive bids?

서양 · 키케로, 무너지는 공화정 안에서

시대적 배경

기원전 1세기의 로마는 이미 지중해 전체를 다스렸지만, 그 제도는 도시국가 시절의 것이었다. 해마다 선출되는 정무관, 원로원, 민회가 서로를 견제하는 구조는 거대한 군대를 거느린 장군들 앞에서 흔들렸다. 키케로(Marcus Tullius Cicero, 기원전 106–43)는 귀족 가문 출신이 아닌 ‘신인(novus homo)’으로 변론 실력만으로 기원전 63년 집정관에 올랐고, 그해 카틸리나 음모를 진압했다. 그러나 몇 해 뒤 그 처리 방식이 문제가 되어 망명을 떠났다가 돌아왔고, 정치의 중심에서 밀려난 기원전 50년대 중반에 『국가론』(De re publica)과 『법률론』(De legibus), 『연설가론』(De oratore)을 썼다. 그는 기원전 43년, 안토니우스의 숙청 명단에 올라 살해되었다.

핵심 사상가와 개념

『국가론』 1권에서 대화의 주인공 스키피오는 공화정을 “인민의 것(res populi)”이라 정의하고, 인민이란 아무렇게나 모인 무리가 아니라 “법에 대한 합의와 이익의 공유로 결합된(iuris consensu et utilitatis communione sociatus)” 집단이라고 말한다(1.39). 정당성의 근거가 왕의 혈통도 신탁도 아니라 법에 대한 합의에 놓인다. 스키피오는 왕정·귀족정·민주정이 각각 타락하기 쉽다고 보고, 셋을 섞은 정체를 가장 낫다고 본다. 이 혼합정체론은 폴리비오스가 로마 정체를 분석한 논의를 잇는다는 해석이 일반적이다.

자연법은 『법률론』 1권에 가장 분명하다. 법은 올바른 이성(recta ratio)과 같은 것이고(1.23), 인간은 정의를 위해 태어났으므로 정의는 그 자체로 추구되어야 한다. 『국가론』 3권에도 ‘참된 법(vera lex)’에 관한 유명한 대목이 있었는데, 이 부분은 원문이 아니라 후대 교부 락탄티우스의 인용으로 전한다. 『국가론』 자체가 오랫동안 단편으로만 알려졌다가, 1819년 안젤로 마이가 바티칸 도서관의 양피지 아래에서 아우구스티누스의 시편 주해 밑에 지워진 채 남아 있던 본문을 찾아냈다. 우리가 읽는 키케로의 정치철학은 그 자체로 지워졌다 되살아난 텍스트다.

철학과 웅변

키케로는 스스로 아카데미아 회의주의를 따른다고 했다. 확실성은 얻을 수 없고, 더 그럴듯한 견해를 고를 수 있을 뿐이며, 그래서 대화 형식으로 여러 입장을 늘어놓고 독자가 판단하게 한다. 자연법을 말하면서도 스토아 학파의 교설을 완결된 체계로 받아들이지는 않았다. 플라톤의 『고르기아스』가 수사학을 단순한 설득 기술로 깎아내린 데 반해, 키케로는 철학이 공동체에 닿으려면 웅변이 필요하다고 보았다. 강의자의 생각으로는, 이것이 키케로를 읽는 열쇠다. 그에게 자연법은 법정과 원로원에서 말로 설득되어야 하는 것이었다.

철학적 의미

“법은 다수결이나 권력자의 명령보다 높은 이성에 답해야 한다”는 생각은 이후 중세와 근대의 자연법 전통으로 이어졌다는 것이 통설이다. 동시에 비판도 가능하다. 키케로의 ‘인민’은 실제로는 시민권을 가진 남성, 그중에서도 원로원 계급의 질서를 지키는 쪽에 기울어 있었다. 보편적 이성의 언어가 특정 계층의 질서를 지키는 데 쓰일 수 있다는 점까지 함께 읽어야 한다.

동아시아 · 동중서, 하늘과 사람이 서로 응한다

시대적 배경

진(秦)이 법가로 천하를 통일했다가 15년 만에 무너진 뒤, 한(漢)은 초기에 황로(黃老) 사상에 기대어 백성을 쉬게 했다. 무제(武帝, 재위 기원전 141–87)에 이르러 제국은 흉노와의 전쟁, 중앙집권, 관료 선발이라는 과제를 함께 떠안았다. 무제는 즉위 초 학자들에게 통치의 원리를 묻는 책문(策問)을 내렸고, 동중서(董仲舒)는 이에 세 차례 답했다. 이 대책들은 후한의 반고(班固)가 지은 『한서』 「동중서전」에 실려 전하며, 흔히 ‘천인삼책(天人三策)’이라 부른다. 동중서의 생몰년은 기원전 2세기로 추정되지만 정확한 연도는 자료마다 다르다.

핵심 사상가와 개념

대책의 첫머리에서 동중서는 『춘추』에 기록된 과거의 일을 살펴 “하늘과 사람이 서로 관여하는 즈음을 보면 매우 두렵다(以觀天人相與之際，甚可畏也)”고 말한다. 이어 나라가 도를 잃으려 하면 하늘이 먼저 재해를 내어 꾸짖고(災害以譴告之), 그래도 반성하지 않으면 괴이한 일로 경고하며, 그래도 바뀌지 않으면 패망이 온다고 썼다. 이것이 천인감응(天人感應)과 재이설(災異說)의 핵심이다. 하늘은 침묵하는 운명이 아니라 통치를 지켜보고 반응하는 질서이며, 홍수와 일식은 정치에 대한 논평이 된다. 같은 글에서 그는 “도의 큰 근원은 하늘에서 나오니, 하늘이 변하지 않으면 도 또한 변하지 않는다(道之大原出於天，天不變，道亦不變)”고 썼다.

구체적 텍스트: 유학의 ‘국교화’를 다시 읽기

세 번째 대책 끝에서 동중서는 육예(六藝)와 공자의 학술에 들지 않는 것은 “모두 그 길을 끊어 함께 나아가지 못하게 하라(皆絕其道，勿使並進)”고 건의했고, 반고는 이를 두고 그가 “공씨를 밝히고 백가를 물리쳤다(推明孔氏，抑黜百家)”고 평했다. 흔히 쓰는 ‘파출백가 독존유술(罷黜百家 獨尊儒術)’은 이 평가를 후대에 압축한 표현이다. 무제 때 오경박사(五經博士)가 설치되어 유학 경전이 관학의 중심이 된 것은 사실이지만, 현대 연구에서는 대책의 작성 연대, 그리고 한 사람의 건의로 유학이 ‘국교’가 되었다는 서술이 과장되었다는 해석이 있다. 동중서의 이름으로 전하는 『춘추번로(春秋繁露)』도 여러 사람의 손을 거친 문헌이라는 것이 학계의 일반적 견해다.

철학적 의미

천인감응은 황제를 하늘의 아들로 높이는 동시에, 하늘의 이름으로 황제를 비판할 길도 열었다. 재해를 해석하는 사람은 경전을 읽는 학자였기 때문이다. 강의자의 생각으로는, 이것은 권력의 정당화이자 견제의 장치라는 이중성을 지닌다. 다만 무엇이 하늘의 경고인지를 정하는 권한이 해석자에게 넘어가고, 다른 학파의 목소리를 ‘함께 나아가지 못하게’ 하는 배제와 짝을 이루었다는 점도 같은 무게로 보아야 한다.

Who acts? Four units, four answers to the question of agency

“Agency” is the most overworked word in this year’s briefs, and software has just claimed it too: an AI “agent” now books flights and writes code. Read side by side, the units do not agree on what the word means. For RCA ADS0 agency is a staged event; for Bartlett PG21 it belongs to things and recipes; for AA Dip2 it is political, held by a neighbourhood; for Bartlett PG23 it sits in matter itself. Choosing between them is choosing a portfolio.

ADS0: Steve Salembier, Elin Eyborg Lund, Louis-Philippe Van Eeckhoutte (RCA, 2025–26) · PG21: Abigail Ashton, Tom Holberton, Andrew Porter (Bartlett, 2025–26) · Dip2: Torange Khonsari, Mehrdad Seyf (AA, 2026–27)

The lineage

1 · Why the word, why now

For most of the twentieth century “agency” was a sociologist’s word, set against “structure”: how far can people act, and how far are they acted upon by class, institution and habit? Architecture borrowed it late, and mostly in a defensive mood. When Nishat Awan, Tatjana Schneider and Jeremy Till published Spatial Agency: Other Ways of Doing Architecture (Routledge, 2011), the argument was that a building is not always the best answer to a spatial problem, and that the architect’s agency might lie in organising, negotiating or refusing to build. The book catalogued practices rather than projects. That is the first sense these briefs inherit: agency as the capacity of the designer to change a situation, not merely to deliver an object.

The briefs of 2025–27 push further, and in two opposite directions at once. One direction turns agency outward, into events and politics. The other turns it towards things, materials and now machines. The arrival of “agentic AI” in a Bartlett brief is the clearest sign that the word has stopped being a term of praise for socially engaged architects and has become a live philosophical problem again.

2 · Agency as a staged event: RCA ADS0

ADS0 calls itself simply Agency. Its brief asks students to design a staged intervention in an ongoing reality, an act that produces a katastrophē, the turn in a drama when the situation flips. The brief names Alfred Hitchcock’s handling of suspense and Henri Cartier-Bresson’s decisive moment. Agency here is a matter of timing. The architect does not hold power over a site; they choose the instant and the place where a small act will tip a situation, and the work is tested at 1:1 and in performance.

The lineage is theatrical rather than sociological. Katastrophē comes from the vocabulary of Greek tragedy, and the unit’s interest in performance and scenography connects it to participatory and situational art since the 1960s. The risk is obvious: a well-timed event can be beautiful and change nothing. A strong ADS0 portfolio has to show what the situation was before and after, not only the moment itself.

3 · Agency of things: Bartlett PG21 and the actant

PG21’s Recipe & Agency starts from the opposite end. Its brief imagines architecture that keeps adjusting itself in an age of agentic AI, and asks for drawings, models, machines and films that work like recipes or manuals, open to endless fine-tuning (the brief’s messa a punto). The agents are not only the designers but the instructions, tools and materials themselves.

The theory behind this is the most contested in the field. Bruno Latour’s actor-network theory, set out for a general reader in Reassembling the Social (Oxford University Press, 2005), proposed that the social is not a substance but a set of associations, and that non-humans (tools, devices, microbes) take part in those associations as “actants”. Earlier, the anthropologist Alfred Gell, in Art and Agency: An Anthropological Theory (Clarendon Press, 1998), had argued that art objects should be studied as a kind of action, carrying and distributing the intentions of persons. Jane Bennett’s Vibrant Matter: A Political Ecology of Things (Duke University Press, 2010) went further, arguing that agency always belongs to an assemblage of human and non-human parts.

The standard objection follows directly. If agency is everywhere, who is accountable? A flat ontology, in which a person and a pipe are both actants, is good at describing how a system behaves and weak at saying who should answer for it. PG21 has to face this objection more urgently than most, because an agentic AI is precisely a thing to which people are tempted to hand responsibility.

4 · Political agency: AA Dip2

AA Dip2, Digital Twins: Contemporary Civic Architecture, uses “political agency” in a sense much closer to the older sociological one. Its brief proposes a social digital twin: not a sensor-fed replica of a city, but objects for conversation, VR and an AI design diary that connect a neighbourhood’s relationships and memories across physical and digital space, built through grassroots organising and ethnography. The question is whose capacity to act is increased. Here the digital is a tool for people, not an agent in its own right. Set beside PG21, Dip2 is a useful check: the same technologies can be used to distribute agency to things or to gather it back to a community.

5 · Agency of matter: Bartlett PG23

PG23’s Recursive Operations (read in this paper on 13 November for recursion) lists “agency of matter” and “error and tolerance” among its concerns. It doubts the linear path from drawing to model to building, and treats the exchange between matter and media as the site of design. Agency here is modest and physical: a cast that slumps, a joint that does not close, a scan that misreads a surface. The material answers back, and the project is how the architect listens.

6 · What a portfolio has to prove

Each reading sets a different burden of proof. ADS0 asks for evidence of change: a before and after, a record of the people who were there. PG21 asks for an instruction that others, human or machine, can run, and a record of what happened when they did. Dip2 asks for evidence of who was given a voice, and how. PG23 asks for the trace of the material’s resistance. Across all four, the weakest portfolio uses “agency” as a compliment to its own intentions. The strongest one draws the moment where something other than the author acted, and says plainly who answers for the result.
