Front page Housing · Viability · Planning · England

1,539 homes approved in Slough with no affordable housing — and both the developer and the council’s own assessor say the scheme is still unviable

The Architects’ Journal (2 November) reports that Slough’s planning committee unanimously approved Muse’s £640 million North West Quadrant, designed by GRID Architects and TP Bennett, on Homes England land. The officer’s report concedes that the absence of affordable housing “tempers the benefits of the proposal”. The arithmetic of viability, not the drawings, decided what the town centre will contain.

The scheme puts 501 build-to-rent homes and 325 co-living studios, among others, in blocks of up to 16 storeys on a 3.6-hectare former university campus, with a 25,000m² office block, 3,220m² of retail, a nursery and a 420-space multistorey car park. Muse puts the viability deficit at £170.5 million; the council’s independent assessor, BPS, put it at £160.8 million and also accepted that the scheme is unviable. Section 106 viability reviews at identified stages are proposed, and Homes England’s Ian Knight suggested grant from the Social and Affordable Homes Programme might later help.

The site’s history explains the weight placed on delivery: the council bought it in 2017, a masterplan followed in 2020, plans were shelved in 2021, and Homes England acquired the land in 2023 before agreeing a deal with Muse. The scheme will be built in three phases over up to 12 years. The AJ adds that Landsec shelved a 1,700-home Studio Egret West scheme in Lewisham last month on viability grounds. For a student of the Diploma, the question is what an architect can still design once the financial model has already fixed the tenure.

CRIT · Question of the day

When a viability appraisal can switch affordable housing to zero while the elevations stay the same, what exactly is the architect’s drawing evidence of? And what would you draw so that the cost of a missing tenure becomes as visible as the height of a block?

  1. Take the Slough scheme’s published figures (501 build-to-rent, 325 co-living, 1,539 in total) and draw a single bar showing tenure mix against a 25-year timeline. Mark who holds the risk in each phase.
  2. Write three lines you would add to a planning drawing set so that a councillor, not an architect, could see what the viability deficit has removed.

Discourse · Biennale · Europe

England · Profession · Labour (3 Nov)

A union writes to every practice with more than ten staff: from January, employers must tell workers they may join one

The Architects’ Journal (3 November) reports that the Section for Architectural Workers (SAW), a Unite branch that calls itself the only trade union specifically for architectural workers in the UK, has sent a mailshot to practices with more than ten employees. From January, under the Employment Rights Act 2025, employers must give new starters a written, “clear” and “neutral” statement of their right to join a union alongside the contract, and remind existing staff by 5 April.

The government will supply the standard wording, which employers may supplement but not amend. The AJ also lists changes due next year, including a cut in the qualifying period for unfair-dismissal claims from two years to six months and tighter fire-and-rehire rules. Whatever one thinks of unions, the statutory duty turns the profession’s long hours, unpaid overtime and portfolio-driven hiring into something a practice must now put in writing.

CRITIf every practice must now state a worker’s right to organise, what in your own drawing office would change first: the hours, the credit lines, or the way a junior is asked to stay late? Draw one week of a studio timetable and mark each point where a statutory right could apply.

Italy · Milan · Stadium · [Background] (30 Oct)

[Background] Foster + Partners shows its replacement for San Siro: a 71,500-seat stadium inside a 281,000m² regeneration

This item pre-dates the last issue and is carried as background because no newer continental story could be confirmed. The Architects’ Journal (30 October) reports that Foster + Partners, working with Manica, released first images of a shared stadium for AC Milan and Inter Milan: a concrete X-frame structure with a double-curved roof carrying photovoltaics, to sit west of the existing ground. Construction is expected to start in 2028, subject to approval, with first matches hoped for in 2031/32.

The old San Siro, built in the 1920s and extended in the 1950s and 1980s, is the stadium the clubs agreed with the city to buy in the previous September. A 2021 scheme by Populous was scrapped amid delays, partly because of the threat of a heritage listing. Norman Foster called the project “a remarkable opportunity to build upon San Siro’s legacy”; the AJ’s report gives no view yet on what will happen to the existing building.

CRITIf a century-old stadium is replaced beside itself and its “spirit” is carried over, what exactly is being conserved: the structure, the ritual, or the land value? Sketch the site plan twice, once with the old ground kept and once without, and note which version the regeneration needs.

AI · Technology · Infrastructure

England · Budget · Data centres · [Background] (28 Oct)

[Background] A “data centre covenant” in the Budget: local jobs and water protection in return for faster grid connections

The Architects’ Journal (28 October), listing five Budget points for architects, reports a new “data centre covenant” that would require data-centre projects to invest in local communities, create local jobs and protect local water supplies. In return, critical data centres would get grid connections and could build on-site generation while they wait. This paper carries it as background because it is the only infrastructure item for AI that it could verify at source this week.

The same list reports an extra £1.5 billion for the Social and Affordable Homes Programme, taking the ten-year total above £40 billion, and a nationally unchanged building-regulations regime this Parliament unless safety requires otherwise. Read together, the Budget treats housing and compute as competing claims on the same grid, water and land.

CRITIf a covenant makes a data centre pay for local jobs and water, is the building an infrastructure or a neighbour? Draw its site as a diagram of what enters (power, water) and what leaves (heat, jobs), and mark which flows the covenant actually names.

물이 차오른 도시, 하늘에서 쏟아진 물: 마이애미는 기록적인 연속 침수를 끝냈고 플로리다 중부 해안에는 응급 홍수 경보가 내려졌다

Yale Climate Connections(11월 2일 게재, 3일 갱신)에 따르면 마이애미는 11월 1일 일요일에 ‘보통(Moderate)’ 수준 해안 침수가 11일 연속 이어진 기록을 끝냈다. 이 중 이틀은 ‘심각(Major)’ 단계였고, 연속 일수는 마이애미 관측 사상 최장이다. 올해 ‘보통’ 이상 침수일은 15일로 종전 기록 10일(2019년)을 넘었으며, 1994년 이후 마이애미의 최고 수위 기록 네 건이 올해 10월 킹타이드(king tide) 때 나왔다. 마이애미의 해수면은 1931년 이후 약 1.12피트 올랐고, 1931년에는 10년에 한 번 일어나던 수준의 침수가 9월 25일부터 11월 2일 사이에만 24일 있었다. 같은 글에 따르면 일요일부터 월요일 정오까지 플로리다 중부 해안 일부에 15~20인치의 비가 내렸고, 미 기상청(NWS)은 웨스트멜버른과 팜베이에 홍수 응급 경보를 발령했다. 11월 23~26일 다음 킹타이드에 강한 바람이 겹치면 다시 침수가 이어질 수 있다고 한다. 강우량은 레이더 분석 기준이라 우량계 값과 다를 수 있다고 필자들이 밝혔다. 이 지면은 이 한 건의 원문만 열어 확인했고, 두 번째 매체로 교차 확인하지 못해 제목에는 수치를 쓰지 않았다.

계산이 먼저 정하는 1층

한쪽은 물이 드는 날 수가 1층의 값을 바꾸고, 다른 쪽은 사업성 계산이 주택의 종류를 먼저 정한다. 어느 쪽이든 도면에 나오지 않는 ‘계산’이 땅의 용도를 정한다. 그 계산표를 도면의 한 장으로 끌어올려 그려 보는 프로젝트가 될 수 있다. 편집자의 가설이며 두 사안은 이어지지 않는다.
