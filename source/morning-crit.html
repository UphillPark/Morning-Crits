Front page Housing · Quality · Profession · England

“We can’t just be knocking up homes”: housing campaigner Kwajo Tweneboa tells a room of top practices to put quality before targets

The Architects’ Journal (27 October) reports that Tweneboa, who began exposing poor housing conditions in 2021, urged architects at an AJ100 lunch club in King’s Cross to resist pressure to prioritise housebuilding targets over quality, to challenge unsafe decisions, and to judge housing by how well it serves the people who live in it. It is the newest substantive item in a quiet weekend for London’s trade press, and it is the oldest argument in the housing debate: number or home.

Speaking at The Lighterman on 22 October, Tweneboa told the AJ’s audience that “we can’t just be knocking up homes for the sake of meeting targets”. He asked for closer collaboration between architects and local authorities, arguing that councils need enough resources to assemble the right teams and architects, and to put residents ahead of profit. Asked by Russell Curtis of RCKa what the “first thing we should focus on solving” is, he said it was hard to name a single priority. He also said that officials sometimes engage and sometimes do not, and that this “isn’t party-specific”.

His authority comes from experience rather than office. According to the AJ, his family experienced homelessness twice during his school years, later lived in social housing run by Clarion Housing where they faced cockroaches, mice, damp and mould, and his father died in those conditions shortly before the first Covid lockdown while undergoing cancer treatment. Tweneboa has campaigned for six years and welcomed politicians with first-hand experience of homelessness as a step towards putting people with “lived experience at the front and centre”. The piece is a report of one speaker at one event: it carries no reply from government, councils, Clarion or housebuilders, and this paper has read it through the AJ’s public page.

England · Profession · Planning (27 Oct)

Half of Public Practice’s new cohort will work in planning, the highest share since the scheme began

According to the Architects’ Journal (27 October), Public Practice, the not-for-profit that places built-environment professionals in councils, has named an autumn 2026 cohort of 41 people embedded across 28 local authorities in England. Half hold planning roles (planning policy, local plan management, development management and infrastructure planning), which the organisation says is the highest proportion since the scheme launched nearly ten years ago, and which it links to a “wider focus on planning reform” as the government tries to raise housebuilding. Around half of the intake are qualified architects.

The scale of the programme is worth holding in mind: since 2017 Public Practice has overseen 469 placements in around 110 public sector organisations, and this cohort was part-funded by a salary support programme backed by the housing ministry (MHCLG), as the spring intake was. Croydon hosts the most listed placements, five regeneration and placemaking roles. Chief executive Pooja Agrawal says the cohort “shows how many people want to work in public sector planning”. The AJ piece is built on the organisation’s own announcement; it offers no independent evaluation of what placements change and no government comment.

CRITIf half of a cohort of architects chooses to work inside the planning system, what does that say about where design decisions are now being made? Draw the route one housing scheme takes from sketch to permission, and mark the seat where an architect employed by the council could change it.

[Background] STARTT’s “micro-architectures” open the archaeology behind the Pantheon to the public

Because the weekend brought no new continental story that this paper could confirm, it carries a background item and says so. Per search summaries of Dezeen and ArchDaily coverage, the Italian studio STARTT has used a series of “micro-architectures” to open a previously inaccessible archaeological area behind the Pantheon in Rome. ArchDaily says the project, Pantheon – Micro Architectures for Archaeology, was promoted by the Italian Ministry of Culture as part of a programme launched in 2019, and that this first phase creates a new entrance from the Pozzo del Diavolo behind the Rotunda, giving access to archaeological fabric previously reserved for technical use. Arquitectura Viva dates the project to 2026.

A Dezeen item of 26 October, also seen only as a search summary, reports that readers chose the project as the heritage winner of the Dezeen Awards public vote; the overall winners are due on 16 November. This paper has not opened the original articles, found no critical response, and could not confirm the publication date of the project coverage itself, so treat the details above as second-grade.

Australia · AI · Regulation (26 Oct, per search summary)

Before the rules, a survey: RMIT and Victoria’s architects’ regulator ask how architects actually use AI

Per a search summary of the survey’s own notice (26 October), RMIT University’s School of Architecture and Urban Design, funded by the Architects Registration Board of Victoria, has opened a survey on how architects use AI across design, documentation and marketing. It can be completed online until 15 November, results are expected next year, and the organisers say they want the findings to guide regulatory responses.

It lands on a gap that Dezeen documented in August (per search summary, 25 August): of 26 leading studios that responded, 25 said they actively encourage AI, but only 19 reported formal AI policies, and six, including MAD, encouraged use without one. The Victorian survey has no results yet, so nothing here shows what practice looks like; what it shows is that regulators are asking the question after the tools are already in the studio.

CRITIf a regulator will one day ask what you used AI for, what would your own portfolio have to disclose today? Write the three-line statement you would attach to a Diploma project: which images, which text, and which decisions were yours.

In brief · London trade and design
  • A breathable house in Suffolk: Studio Bua’s hempcrete self-build wins planning approval. The AJ’s “Material changes” series (30 October) reports a timber-frame house with hemp-lime walls, clad in local flint and reclaimed red brick, for a client who wanted the comfort of her listed cob and thatch cottage. The stated benefits are low embodied carbon, breathability and healthy indoor humidity. The architects say services must be fixed before site work because electrics will be cast into the walls “before the first spray”; they chose durability over easy deconstruction, and a multi-unit version would need an “enlightened client or forward-thinking council”. The piece gives no floor area, budget or timetable.

  • Brew Brick: Studio Egret West fires spent coffee grounds into clay. The AJ (29 October) says the pavilion for Clerkenwell Design Week used about 700 bricks made with some 300kg of grounds collected from local cafés in a week; the brick is about 5% lighter than a conventional one because the organic material burns away in firing, leaving a more porous body that also needs less energy to fire. For now it is limited to interior and non-loadbearing use while durability and certification are tested; the pavilion has been dismantled and its parts redistributed.

DAY 010에서 플라톤은 『국가』에서 나라의 질서와 영혼의 질서를 나란히 놓았고, 맹자는 사람의 마음에서 왕도정치의 근거를 찾았다. 오늘은 질문의 규모가 한 나라에서 우주 전체로 커진다. 같은 날 실린 두 이야기는 비교의 재료이지 서로 영향을 주고받은 관계가 아니다.

서양 · 장인이 모범을 바라보며 만든 세계

시대적 배경

『티마이오스』는 플라톤의 대화편이고, 전통적으로 후기 저작으로 분류한다. 한때 중기 저작이라는 주장도 있었지만, 이후의 문체 연구와 텍스트 연구가 대체로 전통적 연대를 뒷받침했다고 스탠퍼드 철학 백과사전은 정리한다. 이 대화편에서 우주의 생성을 설명하는 긴 이야기는 티마이오스라는 인물이 맡는다. 그래서 글의 무게가 질문과 반박보다 한 사람의 긴 설명에 실려 있다. 이 대화편이 오래 읽힌 이유 하나는, 4세기 칼키디우스의 라틴어 번역이 12세기까지 라틴 서방에서 읽을 수 있던 유일한 플라톤 저작이었다고 백과사전이 적는 점이다.

핵심 사상가와 개념

티마이오스의 설명에서 세계를 만든 존재는 데미우르고스(장인)다. 이 장인은 무에서 세계를 불러내지 않는다. 이미 있는 무질서한 것에, 영원히 변하지 않는 모범(paradeigma)을 바라보며 수학적 질서를 부여한다. 스탠퍼드 백과사전은 이 장인을 지성(nous)을 인격으로 그린 표현으로 읽는 해석을 소개한다. 여기에 세 번째 요소가 더해진다. 모범과 그 모상(images) 외에 티마이오스가 ‘세 번째 종류’라고 부르는 수용자(chōra, receptacle)다. 이것은 스스로는 아무 성격도 갖지 않고, 네 원소가 남기는 자취를 일시적으로 받아들이는 바탕이다. 질료인지 공간인지, 둘 다인지는 지금도 학자들이 다툰다.

장인은 먼저 같음(Sameness)과 다름(Difference)과 있음(Being)을 섞어 세계영혼을 만들고, 그것을 서로 교차하는 두 개의 원으로 나눈다. 이어 몸을 만든다. 불·공기·물·흙 네 원소에 각각 정다면체가 대응한다. 정사면체는 불, 정팔면체는 공기, 정이십면체는 물, 정육면체는 흙이다. 정십이면체는 우주 전체에 쓰인다고 하는데, 구에 가장 가깝기 때문이다. 불·공기·물은 면이 반 정삼각형으로 이루어져 서로 바뀔 수 있지만, 흙은 다른 삼각형으로 이루어져 이 변환에서 빠진다는 설명이 따른다. 티마이오스가 왜 하필 그 삼각형들을 쓰는지는 끝내 설명하지 않는다는 점을 백과사전은 짚는다.

구체적 텍스트와 사례

가장 읽어 둘 대목은 티마이오스가 자기 설명을 스스로 규정하는 부분이다. 그는 우주가 변하는 모상이므로 그 설명도 엄밀한 확실성을 가질 수 없고, 다만 그럴듯한 이야기(eikōs mythos, likely account)일 수 있다고 말한다. 이것이 한계이자 기준이다. 해석은 갈린다. 세계가 시간 속에서 실제로 시작되었다고 읽을 것인가, 구조를 설명하는 비유로 읽을 것인가. 아리스토텔레스는 문자 그대로 읽으면 시간 자체에 시작이 있어야 한다고 반박했고, 옛 아카데미아에서 플로티노스에 이르는 플라톤주의자들 사이에서는 비유로 읽는 해석이 우세했다고 한다. 지금도 이 문제로 학자들은 갈린다.

철학적 의미

이 대화편이 후대에 남긴 가장 큰 유산은 ‘세계는 수학적 구조를 지녔다’는 믿음이다. 동시에 한계도 분명하다. 장인이 바라보는 모범이 어디에 있는지, 지성과 세계영혼은 어떻게 다른지, 수용자는 무엇인지는 대화편 안에서 완전히 풀리지 않는다. 입문자라면 이 미해결 부분을 약점으로만 읽지 말고, 플라톤이 질서를 ‘주어진 사실’이 아니라 ‘설명해야 하는 과제’로 다뤘다는 증거로 읽어 보자.

추연(鄒衍)은 전국시대 제(齊)나라 사람이다. 그의 저작은 모두 사라졌고, 생애와 사상은 『사기』와 다른 초기 문헌의 인용을 통해서만 전한다. 생몰 연대도 자료마다 다르다. 기원전 305~240년경으로 적는 책이 있고, 브리태니커 계열 항목은 기원전 340~260년경으로 추정한다. 사마천은 『사기』 「맹자순경열전」에서 그에게 맹자나 순자보다 더 많은 지면을 쓴다. 제나라 사람들이 “하늘을 말하는 이는 추연”이라 칭송했다고 그 열전은 전한다. 따라서 오늘 우리가 아는 ‘추연’은 사마천의 눈을 거쳐 남은 모습이라는 점을 잊지 말자.

핵심 사상가와 개념

추연은 음양과 오행을 하나의 변화 모형으로 엮은 사람으로 평가받는다. 일부 연구는 음양이 바탕이 되는 ‘성(性)’이고 오행은 그것이 드러나는 ‘질(質)’이라는 틀로 이 결합을 설명한다. 오행은 목·화·토·금·수 다섯 가지 국면(phase)이다. 흔히 ‘다섯 원소’라고 번역하지만 서로를 낳고 이기는 과정을 가리킨다는 점에서 국면이나 과정에 더 가깝다는 지적이 있다. 순환에는 두 가지 방향이 있다. 하나는 상생(相生)으로 목→화→토→금→수의 순서다. 다른 하나는 상극(相克)으로 목→토→수→화→금의 순서다.

이 틀은 계절, 방위, 색, 맛, 장기, 행성 등 많은 것을 분류하는 데 쓰였다. 한대(漢代)에는 이런 상관적 우주론이 학문 전반을 지배했다고 스탠퍼드 백과사전은 쓴다. 『여씨춘추』(기원전 239년경 편찬)의 계절 대응이 그 예다.

구체적 텍스트와 사례

추연의 이름과 가장 단단히 묶이는 두 가지는 『사기』가 전하는 것이다. 첫째는 왕조 교체를 오행의 덕(德)이 차례로 이어지는 과정으로 설명한 점이다. 각 덕은 한 왕조에 대응하고, 하늘이 덕에 따라 통치자를 정한다. 훗날 여러 왕조가 이 모형을 자기 정통성을 세우는 데 가져다 썼다. 둘째는 땅의 구조다. 추연은 중국을 ‘적현신주(赤縣神州)’라 부르고, 그것이 세계의 아홉 큰 땅 중 하나일 뿐이며 각 땅 사이는 바다로 막혀 있다고 했다고 전한다. 이 이야기는 ‘중심은 하나가 아니다’라는 감각을 보여 주지만, 이를 곧바로 근대적 세계 인식과 연결하는 것은 과장이다.

철학적 의미

추연의 도식은 질서를 만든 존재를 묻지 않는다. 오행에서는 변화 자체가 규칙을 지니고, 그 규칙을 잘 읽는 사람이 때를 안다. 한편 이 모형이 정치에 쓰였을 때 무엇이 생겼는지도 함께 봐야 한다. ‘자연의 순환’이라는 이름으로 왕조의 교체와 통치의 정당성이 설명되었다. 철학이 우주를 설명하는 일과 권력을 설명하는 일이 어떻게 맞닿는지 보여 주는 사례다.

Care as a drawing problem: what Dip8 asks a retrofit to prove

Dip8 does not ask for a building that is about care. It asks whether an obsolete building in a Mediterranean city can actually host caring activities, and what in a portfolio would convince a tutor that it does. This sheet reads the brief against the theory it quietly relies on.

Tutors: Nuria Alvarez Lombardero, Francisco González de Canales

The brief, as summarised

The AA’s unit page, which at the time of checking carried the 2025–26 date, describes a growing care crisis around the Mediterranean: too few spaces and services for migrants, refugees and asylum seekers, older people, children, and communities under social, economic or climate pressure. Students study obsolete buildings in Mediterranean cities and test whether those buildings can host care, grounded in local neighbourhoods through the principle of local proximity. The design focus is shared space: connecting passages, courtyards, mediating thresholds, places where everyday activities become shared practices. The stated aim is to turn an existing building into a place for intersectional care, treating retrofit as healing, preservation and historical continuity rather than only a real-estate strategy. The unit page points to a longer extended brief for the full text. I have not quoted it here because I could not read it verbatim.

The lineage

1 · A definition wide enough to hold a building

The most quoted academic definition of care comes from Berenice Fisher and Joan Tronto, in the chapter “Toward a Feminist Theory of Caring”, published in Circles of Care, edited by Emily Abel and Margaret Nelson (SUNY Press, 1990, pp. 36–54). They describe caring as a species activity that includes everything we do to maintain, continue and repair our world so that we can live in it as well as possible; that world includes bodies, selves and environment. The wording circulates in slightly different verb orders, so treat any exact quotation of it as something to check against the original. What matters for an architecture student is the grammar: maintain, continue, repair. Those are verbs that a building undergoes for decades, not once at handover.

The same authors are often credited with breaking care into phases, from noticing a need to receiving help. That is secondary-source shorthand, and the useful reading for a unit like Dip8 is spatial rather than doctrinal: each phase asks where it happens and who is in the room.

2 · Maintenance as a position against “development”

In 1969 the artist Mierle Laderman Ukeles wrote what became known as the “Manifesto for Maintenance Art 1969!”. It began as a proposal for an exhibition called Care. She wrote it as an established New York artist and a new mother, and it divides the work of a city into two systems: development, the new, the progressive and the exciting, and maintenance, the repeated labour that keeps things going. It links the low status of domestic work to the low pay of public maintenance jobs. Her later work with the New York Department of Sanitation grew out of this, though sources differ on the start year of that residency, between 1976 and 1978. For a studio brief, the lesson is concrete: a retrofit drawing that shows only the finished state has drawn the development half of the building and left out the half that keeps it alive.

3 · Care that does not stop at the human

María Puig de la Bellacasa’s Matters of Care: Speculative Ethics in More Than Human Worlds (University of Minnesota Press, 2017, Posthumanities series) argues that care is not only a human activity and follows how it moves through more-than-human worlds. This is a pressure on Dip8’s courtyards and passages. If care includes soil, water, materials and the building fabric itself, then a “mediating threshold” is not only a place where two groups meet. It is also a place where a wall, a drain or a roof is looked after.

4 · The argument the brief invites

The brief is explicit that retrofit should not be read only as real estate. That is a position, and it can be argued against: care programmes also appear in developer proposals, because a clinic or a community kitchen raises a site’s social value. The editor’s reading is that Dip8’s test is therefore a test of evidence. A proposal that says it cares has to show who is cared for, by whom, on which days, and what happens to the building when funding thins. Intersectional care, in the brief’s term, adds that those users do not share one set of needs, so one plan cannot be assumed to serve them all.
