Front page Museums · Adaptive reuse · Heritage · City of London

A market hall becomes a city museum: the London Museum opens in Smithfield’s Victorian General Market this Saturday — and leaves behind a Barbican home that is set to be demolished

Dezeen (24 November) reports that Stanton Williams, Asif Khan and Julian Harrap Architects have completed the transformation of the 1880s General Market at Smithfield, which fell into disrepair after it closed in the 1990s, into the new London Museum. The museum calls it the largest dedicated city museum in the world; its own site gives the opening date as 28 November.

Ten years in the making, the opening is the first phase of the museum’s move from the former Museum of London on the Barbican Estate, which Dezeen describes as set to be demolished; the adjacent 1960s Poultry Market is to follow in 2028. The architects removed the shops that once wrapped the trading floor to open up the hall, recasting 42 individual shops as 12 multi-storey units: six hold a restaurant, galleries and learning spaces, and six are left for partner institutions. Julian Harrap Architects’ Lyall Thow describes keeping the traces rather than erasing them: pockets in the brickwork where first-floor joists once sat, and a darkened band, deliberately not over-cleaned, marking a lost second floor.

Asif Khan frames the strategy as an argument about the institution as much as the fabric: the “museum of tomorrow”, he says, is “a coral reef of small institutes that can plug in”. Below ground, the permanent galleries sit at London’s Roman street level, where, in Khan’s account, the building’s “greater thermal inertia” suits precious objects and “loosen[s] the straitjacket around the museum having to act like a museum” in the more market-like spaces above; a six-metre window frames passing Thameslink trains. Project director Alec Shaw says the museum does not want to be “intimidating”, adding that “some of the other museums are quite imposing buildings.” Read after Monday’s National Gallery story, it is a case for reuse made by an institution whose own previous, purpose-built home is the building scheduled to go.

CRIT · Question of the day

If a museum can only become “a different kind of museum” by moving into a market, what does that say about the purpose-built museum it is leaving to be demolished?

Practice · Tools · Sheffield (24 Nov)

“Not Luddites, but definitely more analogue than AI”: a new Sheffield practice pools three careers and builds a sideline in demolition audits and material storage

The Architects’ Journal (24 November) profiles Bord Collective, founded in April 2026 at Sheaf Plate Works by Joe Marshall, Elin Keyser and Michael Jenkins, who met on the master’s course at the Sheffield School of Architecture and worked in London practices before moving back north. They say work in the North is notably lower in volume and fee than in the south, so rather than compete they pooled resources; current projects include a £5 million leisure refurbishment, a £300,000 rural extension and a low-carbon combined heat and power plant for a listed building in Sheffield.

The founders describe their process as “analogue, sustainable and ethical, yet spatially ambitious”, and set it against a tech-driven “faster equals better” mindset. They want to shift value away from “the glossy render” towards the process itself, introducing clients to working drawings, and are developing a side of the business that facilitates construction, demolition audits, material storage and reuse. The practice is run as a shared resource in which time, office space, insurance and software are lent to peers who bring in work they could not deliver alone.

CRITThe AI debate in practice is usually framed as the speed of drawing output. Bord relocates the fee to the parts of the job that happen on site and in storage yards: the audit, the stockpile, the assembly. Is that a refusal of the tool, or a redefinition of what an architect is paid for?

Opinion · Circular economy · Policy (24 Nov)

Where is the circular economy plan? A member of the government’s own taskforce asks why England’s first strategy has never appeared

In an AJ opinion piece (24 November), Libby Peake, who chaired the built environment working group of the independent taskforce appointed to shape England’s first circular economy strategy, writes that members contributed at least 1,500 days to the work before the process “ground to a halt”. The plan, later branded the Circular Economy Growth Plan, has not been published, and she reports “consternation that it might have been quietly canned”; she asks why it has been kicked into the long grass.

Her case rests on construction’s share of the problem: the sector, she writes, accounts for the largest portion of the UK’s material footprint, a quarter of its consumption-based emissions and nearly two thirds of its waste. Citing Green Alliance analysis, she argues that existing techniques could cut upfront material use by over a third and upfront carbon by nearly 40 per cent, and that leaner design could more than offset expected materials inflation on a typical three-bedroom house. Regulation, not goodwill, would “level the playing field” in a conservative, low-margin sector.

CRITToday’s lead is reuse made voluntary by a rich institution with a heritage building to hand. Peake is asking for reuse made compulsory for everyone else. Which one actually changes the stock: the exemplary project, or the rule that would make it ordinary?

World Architecture Festival · Climate · Oslo, Hasselt, Indore (23 Nov)

The “antithesis of the double-loaded corridor”: an Indian university that steps up like a stepwell is WAF’s World Building of the Year, while Oslo and Hasselt take civic and reuse prizes

The AJ (23 November) reports that Prestige University in Indore, by Sanjay Puri Architects, was named World Building of the Year at the World Architecture Festival, held on 18–20 November in Fort Lauderdale. The 31,000m² building steps up to a 28m peak, with roof platforms that act as an open auditorium for up to 9,000 students, and shaded courtyards intended to keep dependence on air conditioning low where temperatures run from 30 to 40°C for eight months of the year.

WAF programme director Paul Finch called it a “daring and confident project which is the antithesis of the double-loaded corridor”, arguing that its manipulation of plan and section could “seed future approaches” combining landscape and learning. Two European schemes with London partners also won: the New Government Quarter in Oslo, by Nordic Office of Architecture and Haptic, took Civic and Community, and the Beguinage of Hasselt in Belgium, by Bovenbouw Architectuur and David Kohn Architects, took Creative Re-use. Foster + Partners’ conversion of the Whiteley in Bayswater won Retrofit.

CRITThe jury rewards section as climate control: a building that cools by its shape rather than its plant. Draw the same 31,000m² as a double-loaded corridor block and compare what each section asks of the machine room.

Dezeen 20×20 · Africa · Thesis practice (24 Nov)

A thesis “at 1:1 scale”: Lesley Lokko’s pick for the next 20 years bought a 99-year lease on Ghanaian farmland and is building an alternative institution from salvaged vernacular materials

Dezeen (24 November) interviews Courage Dzidula Kpodo, nominated by Lesley Lokko for the magazine’s 20×20 series. Kpodo, who graduated from MIT’s master’s in architecture in 2025 with the Arthur Rotch Prize, works across the roles of architect, artist and archivist, and focuses on what he calls the “silences” in prevailing narratives about Ghanaian architecture, challenging the idea that the country’s development must follow a “linear prescription of urbanisation”.

The site of his MIT thesis was an almost five-acre stretch of farmland in Ghana on which he bought a 99-year lease in 2022, and which he now uses as a live site, building with materials salvaged from vernacular buildings that would otherwise be demolished. “The committee at MIT was a bit shocked, because they weren’t used to the idea of a thesis project existing in the real world,” he told Dezeen. “But I still thought of it as almost as a sort of sketch, just at 1:1 scale.”

CRITFor a Diploma applicant this is the sharpest question of the day: what would it take for your thesis to have a real site, a real lease and a real material source? If the answer is “nothing I can afford”, what is the 1:1 fragment you could still build?

연합뉴스(11월 24일)에 따르면 정부는 2031년까지 중앙행정기관 등 31개 기관 소속 공무원 5천460명을 세종으로 우선 이전하는 계획을 고시했다. 방송미디어통신위원회, 개인정보보호위원회 등 22개 기관 1천600명은 민간 건물 등을 임차해 내년까지 먼저 옮기고, 감사원·고위공직자범죄수사처·경찰청 등 별도 청사와 특수시설이 필요한 9개 기관 3천860명은 신축청사를 지어 2031년까지 옮긴다. 지난 3일 공청회 안에서는 내년 임차 이전 대상이었던 인권위(정원 231명)가 신축청사 대상으로 바뀌면서 이전 시기가 늦춰질 전망이다. 기사에 따르면 인권위의 입장문에는 국가인권기구의 독립적 위상을 존중해야 한다는 점과, 세종 이전이 장애인·노인·이주민 등 취약계층의 접근성을 떨어뜨려 권리 구제의 장벽이 될 수 있다는 우려가 담겼다. 외교부·통일부·국방부·법무부 등 법령 개정이 필요한 13개 기관 4천469명은 이번 고시에서 빠졌고, 정부는 법령 개정 뒤 추가로 변경 고시할 계획이다.

기다리는 땅

수도권의 빈 필지는 금융과 분양 전망을 기다리고, 시리아의 밭은 지뢰 제거를 기다린다. 쓸 수 있게 되기 전의 땅을 어떤 순서로, 어떤 임시 구조물로 열어 갈지 — 기다림 자체를 설계 대상으로 삼는 주제로 묶인다.

아이오와 납 아지드 폭파(해외) × 순환경제 계획의 실종(1면)

1면의 Libby Peake는 건설 폐기물을 줄일 계획이 사라졌다고 묻고, 아이오와에서는 낡은 군수 물질을 없애는 방법 자체가 마을의 위험이 됐다. 무언가를 처분할 때 그 결과가 내려앉는 땅까지를 부지로 그리는, 처분의 건축을 다루는 주제가 된다.
